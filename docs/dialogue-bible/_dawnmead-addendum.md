# DAWNMEAD — addendum for THE CONTESTED LANDS

To be merged into `dawnmead.md` when the dialogue branch lands. Three
parts: the replacement for the file's header paragraph ("The town's
shape"), new entries for the places that have no throat and three mouths,
and the canon fixes the plan lists. Nothing here changes a shipped line.
The Ring law, the player paragraph, T1 and the contradictions paragraph
stand exactly as written.

---

## 1. The replacement header

Replace the paragraph beginning **"The town's shape."** with this:

**The town's shape.** Dawnmead is still the calm one. Nothing here is
cosmic. Its trouble is the plainest trouble there is: *people leave, and
mostly they do not write*, and this year the trouble walked in the gate
instead of out of it. Three families off the drowned Fenside are under
sacking against the hedge. A cousin's roof on Cottage Row went up in the
spring and the chimney stands alone. There is a grave on the old road out
with no stone on it yet, and it is not one of Halla's. Two clerks and a
lamp-boy argue on the green every noon about whose fault the road is, and a
survey lead has the inn's best bed. Every adult in this village still keeps
a private archive of the departed and none of them compare notes about it;
now they keep a private account of the year as well, and the accounts do
not agree. Wren keeps a drawer of unsent letters. Ottery keeps a shelf of
first crafts and makes swords this year. Gilly keeps four beds and is making
up floors. Berrit sets an extra place at the long table and feeds three
more families than the table seats. Nobody in Dawnmead calls any of this
grief. They call it the work, and this year there is more of it.

**The village's reading of the year.** Weir says the brook is up a finger
again. Brammel says the rats came back a season early. Halla says one fire
too many. Alder says the pack went north. Varn says the ash sat up. Every
one of them is right about their own ground, none of them puts it in one
room, and **nobody says "the deep"**. The bad year is a bad year to the man
losing sheep, and that is exactly why it lands. Any line that gestures at
something bigger from inside a villager's mouth is a failure of the pass.

## 2. New entries: the places with three mouths

These are not throats. They are scenes with signs, and the sign's voice is
the sign-raiser's, never the narrator's (VOICE.md §5). Each has two or three
village explanations and none is corrected, by anyone, ever.

### THE BURNT COTTAGE — Cottage Row, west
**Sign:** "HOBB'S COUSIN'S ROOF. Went up in the spring. Nobody agrees how."
**Mouths:** `yardmaster_halla`, `farmer_brammel`, `twin_wick`; refusals from
`keeper_wren` and `sparkwright_varn`

**What it is.** A wood-walled cottage burnt to its outline, the south wall
gone, stone at two corners, the roof down inside, the chimney standing
alone at the north wall, and the hearth still breathing a thin smoke by
day and a coal-glow by night, which is the column the Ring sees on a
waker's first morning. Grass through the ash. A cart of salvage in the
lane. It was Hobb's cousin's, built by Hobb before Hobb's family took the
road east to Amberford (see the fix below); the cousin stayed in it, and it
went up on a spring evening, and the cousin walked east after his people
with what the cart would hold. Nobody died in it. The village is clear on
that and on nothing else.

**Halla:** a dusk fire jumped the hedge. She was counting fires from the
knoll and there was one too many and it was hers, on the yard side of the
hedge, banked wrong by a ward she had drilled. She owns it flat and once.
"One of mine jumped the hedge. I count them. That one I counted late."
**Brammel:** the thatch was rotten a year and he said so, to the cousin, to
Hobb before that, and to anyone who asked after. "Rotten a year. I said
so. A roof does not need a spark when it is already halfway to ash."
**Wick:** the sixth stone. "There were six stones once and the sixth one
left, and it went out the back way, and it was hot."
**Wren:** offers no theory about the cottage, exactly as she offers none
about the Ring, and her refusal is her position. "I do not know how it went
up, child, and I have watched people decide things for fifty years."
**Varn:** has been testing the ash on the spark pad's east verge and it
"sat up" when ordinary ash lies down. He has written it down. He does not
know why and does not guess. This is a fact about ash and he keeps it as
one.

**Law.** None of the three is corrected, Wick least of all. Tansy reports
Wick's theory as a crime and does not offer her own. Nobody says the winter.
The cottage is not the winter and is not the Ashlamp; both burned this
spring and the winter was years back.

### THE ROAD ROW — the old-road spur's shoulder
**Sign:** none (the spur's own board stands six rows south; one sign per
eyeful). **Mouths:** `yardmaster_halla`, `innkeep_gilly`, `forester_alder`

**What it is.** Three cut gravestones and one tall under an oak, older
stones freshly cut, and ONE grave mound with no stone on it yet. A rail
with the gap open, a bench, a field cairn where the path meets the spur,
tuft and not flowers. Past it a cold camp where somebody waited a night
and did not go, and the edge woods thinned so the road out reads as a road
into something.

**Whose the new one is.** A crofter's, off the drowned Fenside: one of the
old who did not survive the walk west, come up the old road in a cart
because the cart was already on the old road, and buried at the Row because
the Row is where Dawnmead buries. NOT one of Halla's. Halla lost wakers, not
many, and not recently, and the bible holds that; the plan holds it too.

**Halla:** "Two of mine, years back. I counted them once, out loud." She
means the two older stones, and she will not say the names to a stranger,
and the count is the whole of what she gives.
**Gilly:** "The new one came up the old road in a cart." She made up a floor
for the family that night and knows the name and does not say it, because
it is on Hilde's slate and Hilde reads it at noon.
**Alder:** "Those stones are older than this year. They cut them fresh." He
means the three cut stones and the tall one: the stone is old, the cutting
is new, and somebody paid a mason this year for graves that were already
graves, and he does not say who, because he does not know.

**Law.** Three true things about four stones and one mound, and none of
them is about the same grave, and no throat notices that. Leave it.

### THE SACKING ROW — the East Wold, the hedge's south face
**Sign:** none (the FIRST ROAD board is the eyeful's board). **Mouths:**
the `fenside_crofter` pool (the older woman who cooks), `cook_berrit`,
`innkeep_gilly`, `farmer_brammel` at the Common's gate

**What it is.** Three families under two hide tents and a lean-to against
the hedge between the gate lamps and the water: an ember bed cooking fire
(never a campfire), a trough, a cart of belongings and a broken cart, crates,
a drying rack, litter where a load spilled, a trodden ellipse of dirt. A
day camp; the crofters sleep on the crowded roof at the Row's east end.
From the gate lamps it is the first thing a departing waker sees before the
threshold stones.

**The row's own mouth** (the pool): "The water came up in the night. We
did not hear it. You do not hear water." The fire is small on purpose.
**Berrit:** feeds them and will not be thanked and does not discuss the
winter, which they have never heard of. "Three more bowls. That is not a
number that troubles a pot."
**Gilly:** is making up floors and keeping score with Berrit in her own
head. "Four beds, and I am making up floors. Do not tell Berrit I am
winning."
**Brammel:** at the Common's gate, daily, with the crofter who argues. "His
common is grass. Ours is ash." Brammel's half: "It is grass because I keep
it grass. Move them to the far rail, at least."

**Law.** The refugees are not pure victims (their sheep are on a common
nobody gave them; they will not pay the levy and will not stop asking for
the dike) and the village is not pure host (Gilly wants coin, Brammel
wants his rail). Leif's mam is under the sacking and the pool calls him
"the lamp-boy" and never "ours" in front of a Waykeeper.

### THE OLD ROAD — the spur past the Row
**Sign:** "THE OLD ROAD. Kingsdelf, forty-one stones. Dark past the third.
Ask at the Stone." (in the Returners' voice; Eskil raised it). **Mouths:**
`returner_hilde`, `waykeeper_leif`, `sparkwright_varn`

**What it is.** The one way out of Dawnmead that carries no lamp, worn
hard for thirty rows to the knoll and the graves and then breaking to
alternate-tile dirt with tall grass between. It leaves the south hem at the
muster court and drops south-west toward the Third Stone, Aske's toll
stone, and the digs.

**Hilde:** "It is lit to the third stone. Past that it is lit where
somebody has walked with oil this month. That is the honest way to say it
and I have not found a shorter one."
**Leif:** "Hale says the order does not walk it, so the order does not
lamp it. He says it the same way every time. I have not decided if that is
a rule or a habit."
**Varn:** counts the cairns along it for his errand and holds four theories
across four turn-ins, contradicting, never corrected: somebody is moving
them; the ground is; the diggers stack them wrong on purpose; somebody sets
them right by morning. The fourth is true (Sarsen) and stays a theory.

## 3. Canon fixes (the plan's list, applied)

1. **Rill never walks the trail.** The plan's orchard note says so and the
   bible already says so ("knows the hunters' trail north better than anyone
   alive in this village and does not walk it"). Her rag-stakes and the
   skull at the hedge arch were hung so the sign is read by people who
   cannot read; she hung them from the arch side and did not go past it.
   Her one new line stops at: "The wolves came down to the arch in the
   spring. They do not come to be fed." No routine, bark or quest sends her
   up the trail. Any draft that does is wrong.
2. **Wren theorizes about nothing, the cottage included.** Her refusal to
   theorize about the Ring extends to the burnt roof, the graves, the water
   and the year. Her one `threat_near` line is "Halla is counting again. I
   knit; it comes to the same." She offers no cottage theory and does not
   hear Varn's (the bickering pair Wren vs Varn gains the cottage-ash
   refusal). She is the ONE elder allowed gentle wisdom and hers stays
   practical, never cosmic.
3. **Hobb's family LEFT for Amberford.** They are alive, they walked east
   with a cart before the spring, and Rowan's gate book has them. The
   shipped map comment ("Hobb's people stored grain here before the family
   took the road") is correct and stands. The cottage on the Row was the
   cousin's; the cousin walked east after them when the roof went. Nobody
   named Hobb died and nobody named Hobb is a throat. The board says "HOBB'S
   COUSIN'S ROOF" because Hobb built it and the village names roofs by the
   hand that raised them.
4. **The fresh grave is a crofter's.** The Road Row's one grave mound with
   no stone is one of the drowned-out's old, dead of the walk west, come up
   the old road in a cart. It is NOT one of Halla's wakers: the bible holds
   that she lost wakers "not many, and not recently", and Halla's line about
   the Row ("two of mine, years back") refers to the two older stones. Gilly
   knows the crofter's name and does not say it; Hilde reads it at noon.
5. **Berrit never discusses the winter**, and the new throats' three
   attributions (T46) are theirs, not hers. No new throat conflates the
   winter with the Ashlamp or the cottage.
6. **Nobody explains the Ring**, and the new throats have no Ring theory
   at all: Margit has never seen a waking and does not ask, Hilde came out of
   the stones and does not discuss it, Leif has seen two and counts them
   against Tansy's nine, Steinar calls it "a feature".
