# THE CONTESTED LANDS — the country around Dawnmead, a bad year all round

The six zones inside three hundred tiles of the Waking Ring, and the people
who hold them. Nobody in this file is a villain and nobody in it is clean.
Every party carries a fault and a virtue that both survive being looked at,
and every scar on the ground has two or three mouths that explain it and
none of them agrees. This file is the material for those mouths. Read the
Dawnmead file first (and its addendum, `_dawnmead-addendum.md`): the village
is where the year walked in, and half the throats below stand on its green.

Source of record for the design: `docs/contested-lands-plan.md` §1 (the
laws), §2 (the parties), §3 (the zones), §7 (the village), §11 (the Dolmen),
§13.2 (the re-celled map). Where this file and the plan disagree about a
fact, the plan is wrong and this file is wrong and the lead decides; say so
in your report.

**The corner of the spine this cast holds, and never names.** The water on
the First Road came up and nobody saw it rise. A pack that denned in the
Thornveil forty years came down onto sheep. A kerbed barrow coughed up a
people who had been under it. Stones that had held longer than any count
moved. Every throat below has filed its piece of that under weather, wolves,
wains and bad setting, and every one of them is a competent adult reading
their own ground correctly. **Nobody connects it to anything.** A line that
gestures at "something bigger" from inside a mouth is a failure of the pass.
Brede's "like a bill nobody sent" is the closest anyone comes, and it is
bookkeeping.

**The register.** THE PEOPLE SPEAK: whole sentences, hubs of two or three
sentences, answers under about sixty words and never past ninety, feeling in
proportion to the stakes, no dashes in anything a player reads, one spice per
throat and most throats granted none. Nobody here is a teacher; every fork
is gated past the tutorial's capstone. The pooled throats are short and
repeated: three alternatives beat one speech.

**The closed throat.** Several of these people can shut their door to a
character (Halvor at outlaw band, Eskil's beds, the sentinels, Torsten). A
closed throat still speaks: it gives the refusal line, in the person's own
voice, and nothing else. Write the refusal as carefully as the welcome.

## The green — the three outside voices, the widow, the drowned-out

### Margit — Charter tally-clerk, the tally stall on the green
**Actor:** `charter_margit` (proposed; fordgate member) · **Trees:**
`margit_tally`, `margit_ledger` (new), the corn-carry repeatable's offer and
turn-in, the licence and concession bill nodes read by the west and
north-west forks

**Life.** Ford-born, a rope-walker's daughter off the ferry bank, taught her
letters in the bank's counting room the year Cormund was still paying back
what the chain took. She has clerked for the Charter nine years: two at the
ford, three in Pinewatch under Ebba, four on the road with the survey wains.
She was sent to Dawnmead in the spring to count what the Charter's works cost
and what they earn, and to post both on the green where the village can read
them, because the Charter believes a posted number is a fair number. She
keeps one stall, a lectern, a chest of tally crates, and a ledger that goes
home to the ford every fortnight with whoever walks east. She sleeps in the
worn cottage on Cottage Row and pays Gilly in Charter chits, which Gilly
takes and does not like.

**Wants.** "The Charter's number posted where anyone can read it. If the
number is wrong, somebody will tell me, and then it will be right." She
means it. She has been told, by Leif, every day since she came.

**Carries.** Her tally is the only honest number in the valley, and it is
honest about one thing nobody has asked her: the Copse's cordwood, counted
by the load against two hearths, cannot heat Dawnmead and the Third Stone
both through the cold. She has done the arithmetic four times. She has told
nobody, because it is not her column, and because the day she posts it is
the day the village and the Returners stop being neighbours. **Route:** ask
her what the Copse yields, twice, on two days; the second time she gives the
number and the two hearths in one flat sentence and asks the player not to
carry it to the bench where Hilde sits. She is not cruel. She is a clerk who
has found a number that is going to hurt people and is holding it the way
you hold a hot pan.

**Knows.** The count of carts turned at the fen waist (four; she is wrong or
Leif is, and neither is lying). The levy book, by name, and the drowned who
are on it. The concession chart's lot numbers (Bodil's cut, Steinar's seam,
Ingram's dike) and what each has cost to the penny. That the stone-cart's
forty stones came off a bank with no vendor, entered as "field", and that the
carter-boss Garrow has been asked once where the bank was and gave a place
that is not a place. She counts the Dolmen in the same column as stumps
because the ledger has no other column, and she knows that is a fault of the
ledger and has not fixed it.

**Room.** Ingram (`charter_ingram`) sends her the levy and she posts it;
they are civil and she thinks his dike is a good work billed to the wrong
people. Steinar (`charter_steinar`) is her senior and takes his meals at the
inn; she bills his stopped concession without pleasure if the west fork goes
against him. Bodil (`charter_bodil`) she has met twice and likes. Garrow
(`charter_garrow`) she does not like and has written down why. Leif
(`waykeeper_leif`) is her daily quarrel and the closest thing she has here to
a friend. Hilde (`returner_hilde`) she nods to and does not sit with. Gilly
(`innkeep_gilly`) wants coin. Cormund (`banker_cormund`) taught her the hand
she writes in, at one remove: T4 says Neave clerked under him, and Margit
clerked under Neave's replacement.

**Threads.** T45 (Aldis's fourth duration: her ledger is what `word_on_the_road`
reads). T50 (the stone-cart's forty, with Garrow and Ammat).

**Remembered.** `margit_two_hearths` — set when she gives the cordwood number.
Read in `margit_ledger` (she asks whether it was carried) and by Hilde's tree
(if the player carried it, Hilde has heard, and says so in one line).

**Wit.** None. She is precise, not dry.

**Speaks.**
> "Carts turned at the fen waist this week: four. Lamps out past the gate:
> two. That is the Charter's count and it is posted so you can argue with it."
> "The boy says three and a barrow. I have never once seen him count a
> barrow as a barrow. It is a cart to him if it has a wheel."
> "It is not my column. That is the whole of why I have not said it, and it
> is not a good enough reason, and I know that."

### Hilde — Returner widow, the bench by the bell at noon
**Actor:** `returner_hilde` (proposed; returners member, never a fineActor) ·
**Trees:** `hilde_bench`, `hilde_slate` (new), `hilde_shelf` (gated on the
south fork)

**Life.** She came out of the stones twenty-two years ago and married Audun,
who had come out of them four years before her and could not sit still. When
the Charter banked the Returning and the Old Road filled with wains going
south to Kingsdelf, Audun walked with them, and walked back, and walked
again, and on the fourth walk he did not come back through any gate. A
Returner lamp had gone out at the twentieth stone on a night he was counting
on it. That was six years ago. She keeps the Returners' oil-subscription
slate and reads it aloud on the green at noon so that no name on it is
whispered about, and she keeps a PitLampDark by her own door that she has
not lit since. The middle cottage on the Row is hers, hedge unclipped.

**Wants.** "The road lit to the delf, stone by stone, by anybody's oil. I
do not care whose. Audun did not care whose."

**Carries.** Audun died of exactly the thing the order says a Returner lamp
is: a light somebody trusted that was not there. She knows the doctrine is
true. She reads the subscription anyway, and the subscription is taken from
people who have less than she does, and she knows that too. The dark lamp at
her door is the whole of it: she cannot light it and she cannot take it down.
**Route:** ask about the lamp by her door. She tells you what it is in one
sentence and does not tell you why it is dark. Ask on a second day and she
says the stone's number, and that is all you get, ever.

**Knows.** Every name on the oil slate and what each gave (a copper, a
week's eggs, a coat). That Eskil has walked the road more times than any
living Returner and that he is wrong about the diggers. That Torsten calls
the lamps lies in glass and that he is not wrong either; she will say both in
one breath and not flinch. Her Berrit's-winter attribution, given plainly:
it was the winter the Old Road went dark and nothing came up from the south.
(Margit and Eskil each have a different year and a different cause; leave all
three.) Her Kingsdelf shelf: things that came up the road in Audun's pack
over four walks, kept, and sold one at a time to a character who has lit the
south (the `nine_lit` reward line).

**Room.** Eskil (`returner_eskil`) is her keeper and she is his conscience;
they disagree about the digs and agree about everything else. Leif
(`waykeeper_leif`) argues with her at noon about whether a lamp is a
decision or a weather, and she likes him for arguing. Margit she nods to.
Wren (`keeper_wren`) she sits with sometimes; neither of them mentions Audun
and both of them know the letter in Wren's drawer has his name on it. Gilly
gives her the green cottage's key and takes nothing for it.

**Threads.** T44 (the Returners' far end: Soren's lamps and Liv's count at
Kingsdelf). T46 (Berrit's winter, three attributions).

**Remembered.** `hilde_named_you` — set when the south fork's law branch is
taken; she reads the character's name off the slate aloud, once, and the
tree's hub changes for good.

**Speaks.**
> "I read the names at noon so nobody has to say them quietly. If your name
> is on it, it is on it out loud, and that is the whole of the shame."
> "A lamp is a decision. Weather is what happens to it after. The boy has
> those the wrong way round and I have not managed to turn him."
> "It went out at the twentieth stone. That is the number. I have no more to
> say about the number."

### The Fenside crofters — pooled, titled, no names
**Actor:** `fenside_crofter` (proposed; ×3 bodies: the sacking row, the
crowded roof's pen, the Common's west gate; fenside members) · **Trees:**
none (barks, the sacking-row bark pool, the Common bark pair with Brammel)

**Life.** The drowned-out. Three families off the Fenside crofts on the
First Road whose furrows went under in the spring and did not come back up.
They walked west because west was dry and Dawnmead has a hedge, and they
are living under sacking against the hedge's south face by day and two
families to the east cottage by night, with their sheep on Brammel's common
without asking. Their corn is standing cut green on stilted pallets back at
the crofts, waiting for anyone at all to carry it past Brede's bar. They
speak as a pool and give no name, because the next one you meet is a
different family wearing the same wet.

**Wants.** The mere let down by the old sluice. The crossing free. Their
corn carried. And to go home, which is under water, and they say so as a
place and not a grief.

**Carries.** They are not pure victims, and the pool must not be written as
if they were. Their sheep are on a common nobody gave them. They will not
pay the Charter's levy and they will not stop asking for the Charter's dike.
Halvor's son put an axe through a keep-pool. They know all of it and they hold
it the way people under sacking hold things: as the least of what has
happened to them this year, which is honest and is also convenient.

**Knows.** The crofts as they were, furrow by furrow. That the wall folk on
the south bank counted their sheep onto the dry stone of the Course and let
them stand, and that the sheep are alive because of it; the pool says "the
wall folk" openly and without fear, which is a thing nobody else in the
valley does. That Leif is one of theirs (his mam is under the sacking; the
pool calls him "the lamp-boy" and never "ours" in front of a Waykeeper).

**The three voices.** Three bodies, one pool, but a bark writer needs to
hear three throats or the sacking row reads as one person in three places:
- **The sacking row** (an older woman, the one who cooks): flat, weather
  first, the ember bed is hers and the trough is hers and the rest is not.
  "The fire is small on purpose. Sit if you are sitting."
- **The pen at the crowded roof** (a young man, the one who counts sheep):
  numbers and animals, sour about the Common, fond of the wall folk. "Six
  ewes and the lamb. The stone folk counted them onto the wall and I counted
  them off it. Same number both ways."
- **The Common's west gate** (a man Brammel's age, the one who argues):
  short, tired, will not be lectured about grass. "His common is grass. Ours
  is ash. I will move them when there is somewhere to move them to."

**Room.** Halvor (`fenside_halvor`) is their headman, back at the crofts; they
speak of him as the man who will get the sluice open and do not say what his
son did. Brammel (`farmer_brammel`) at the west gate, daily, the same
argument. Leif, quietly. Berrit feeds them and Gilly counts the floors.
Hilde has two of their names on the slate and reads them like any other.

**Threads.** T47 (the wall folk; Halvor's one private thing).

**Speaks.**
> "The water came up in the night. We did not hear it. You do not hear
> water, that is the trouble with it."
> "The corn is cut. It is standing on boards at the crofts going green
> waiting for somebody with legs. Anybody at all."
> "The wall folk let the sheep stand on their stone. Ask them nothing. They
> will tell you a number."

### Leif — Hale's lamp-boy, the green at midday
**Actor:** `waykeeper_leif` (proposed; waykeepers) · **Trees:** `leif_chalk`,
`leif_lamp` (new); the daily bark pair with Margit on `toll_near`, the noon
pair with Hilde

**Life.** Twelve, or near enough. A Fenside crofter's boy whose family's
furrows went under in the spring; his mam is under the sacking at Dawnmead's
gate and his da is at the crofts with the green corn. Hale took him on at
the First Lamp the week the water came up because a post with four hands
and a lamp needs a fifth hand to carry oil and chalk, and because Hale
counts people in hands and a boy is half of one and that is still more than
none. He walks from the lamp to Dawnmead's green and back every day, chalks
the tithe on the green's LampPost, and stands in front of Margit's stall
while she reads her count so that he can read his.

**Wants.** "To walk the lamps myself. Hale says when I am a whole hand. I
am counting."

**Carries.** He counts three carts and a barrow because the barrow was his
family's, and it went into the ditch dark past the picket lamp the night
the Doorless doused it. He has never said this to Margit, and Margit has never
asked why a boy counts a barrow, and that silence is the whole of their
quarrel. He is not a sad child. He is a busy one who has decided that
counting things properly is how you make them come out right.

**Knows.** Every lamp between the First Lamp and the gate, lit or unlit, by
name and by night. The exact wording of "the lamp stays lit", which he says
like a boy repeating a lock's click. That Hale polishes the brazier before
anything else and that this is correct. That Halla drilled under Hale once
(he has heard Hale say her name the way sergeants say an old comrade's, and
has decided it means something, and is right). He does not know why the
water came up. He has not thought to wonder.

**Room.** Hale (`waykeeper_hale`), above everything. Margit, daily, the four
against the three. Hilde at noon, the decision against the weather. The
crofter pool (his mam; the pool calls him the lamp-boy). Wick and Tansy
(`twin_wick`, `twin_tansy`) count wakings at him and he counts lamps back.
Torsten (`waykeeper_torsten`) he has met once and was frightened of and
would follow anywhere.

**Threads.** T48 (Hale's post made ground; the roads file).

**Speaks.**
> "Three carts and a barrow. The barrow counts. It had a wheel and it had
> corn in it and it went in the ditch, so it counts."
> "Lamps out past the gate: two. I chalk it, she reads it, we disagree about
> the barrow, and then I walk back. That is the whole of my post."
> "The lamp stays lit. Hale says it and then he goes and does it, which is
> the part people leave out."

### Steinar — Charter survey lead, billeted at the Five Stones
**Actor:** `charter_steinar` (proposed; fordgate member) · **Trees:**
`steinar_chart`, `q_stake_the_seam_offer`, `q_stake_the_seam_turnin`; his
shelf (closes on the west fork's crown branch)

**Life.** A Silverfall man, thirty years a surveyor, twelve of them for the
Charter since the Crown stopped paying for charts of country it would not
garrison. He ran the chain for the Kingsdelf Returning's road survey and
never once went down the Old Road himself; he sends chainmen. He came west
in the spring to chain the tin seam on the wold and cut a cart road to it,
and he took the inn's best bed and a table in the common room for his chart
because a survey lead does not sleep under canvas when there is a roof
within a day's walk. His stakes run in a ruled line straight across a barrow
kerb and a goblin's furrows to the seam, and he has not walked out to see
either, because the line is the line.

**Wants.** "The seam chained, the cart road cut, and the tin at the ford
before the cold. Straight lines are cheap. Bent ones are what cost."

**Carries.** He knows his chain crossed the kerb. Rurik's stake proves it
and he has seen Rurik's stake. He has decided that a kerb is stones and a
concession is paper and paper wins, and he has said so to Margit, who wrote
it down, which he did not enjoy. He does not think of the Doorless as a
people. He thinks of them as an encampment on the wrong side of a line, and
the day a player makes him say the word "farm" about them is the day the
scene lands. **Route:** ask what the barrow is. He says "a feature". Ask
what is farming on it. There is a pause and he says "goblins", and then he
says the lot number.

**Knows.** The concession chart, which is the one document in the valley
that says who may cut, chain and dike what, and where. Rurik wants it and
Steinar knows Rurik wants it and keeps it under his hand at the table. That
Ottery needs tin and cannot have the crag's two rocks be more than they are.
That the ground went soft where his fence stopped, and he has not asked why.

**Room.** Rurik (`crown_rurik`), his opposite: a chain that agrees with the
ground against a chain that agrees with the paper. Margit, who bills what he
does. Gilly (`innkeep_gilly`), who has given him the best bed and would like
it back. Ottery (`wright_ottery`), whom he calls "the smith" and who has
corrected him twice. Alder (`forester_alder`) has told him where the kerb is
and been thanked for it and ignored.

**Threads.** T49 (Rurik carried Hedda's chain; Steinar ran the Returning's
road survey; the two Crown-and-Charter chains have crossed before).

**Speaks.**
> "The seam is there. The chain says so and the chain does not have
> opinions. Everything between here and it is the cost of getting there."
> "A kerb is stones. A concession is paper with a seal on it. I have never
> seen stones win that argument, and I have surveyed for thirty years."
> "Goblins. Lot nineteen. Yes, I know what they are doing on it. I am
> telling you what it is called."

## The First Road east — the fen lamp and the bar

### Ingram — dike-master of the causeway head
**Actor:** `charter_ingram` (proposed; fordgate member) · **Trees:**
`ingram_dike`, `q_stakes_in_the_waist_offer`, `q_stakes_in_the_waist_turnin`,
the "obstruction" bill node

**Life.** Born on the Amber below the ford where the river is a working
thing, apprenticed to the water gate the town voted eleven years ago and
took a season to build, and he has built dikes and gates and causeways for
the Charter ever since, at the ford, at the Free Furrows' drain, at
Pinewatch's boom landing for one bad winter. He came west in the spring to
lay a dry road across the Amberfen's waist because the First Road was
drowning at the crofts and a drowned road turns carts back, and turned
carts are the one thing the Charter cannot bill. He lives under canvas at
the drowned crofts, where the dike begins, with a counter, a levy book, an
ochre pennant and a chest, and he is on the dike bank at first light every
day with a level in his hand.

**Wants.** "A dry road to the ford by the cold. Dry, level, and paid for by
the people it keeps dry, which is everybody on it."

**Carries.** His dike drains the mere into the crofters' own furrows, and
he knows it, because he can read water better than anyone in the valley,
and the levy that pays for the dike is billed to the crofters it drowns. He
has written the Charter twice saying the levy should fall on the ford and
not the crofts, and been told the ford's moot voted otherwise. He bills the
drowned. He does not enjoy it. He will not stop, because a dike half built
is worse than no dike, and that is true. **Route:** ask him who pays. He
gives the levy's terms. Ask him who should pay. He says the ford, flat, and
then says the ford voted, and that is the sentence where the man is.

**Knows.** That the water rose before his stakes went in, which he says
plainly and which nobody on the Fenside believes. His reading of why: the
Company broke the old sluice to keep the road slow, "a wet road is a paying
road", and he will say that to Brede's face and has. He has seen the
kelp-string on the sluice post and calls it weed. He has no idea what the
Course is and calls the wall folk "the masons on the south bank" and has
never once asked them for stone, which is the Charter's whole failing in one
man.

**Room.** Margit posts his levy. Halvor (`fenside_halvor`) is the man he has to
bill and the man whose sluice he wants closed for good; they have shouted
twice and shaken hands once. Brede (`first_road_toll` crown) he calls a
thief and pays, because a Charter wain does not fight at a bar. Hale
(`waykeeper_hale`) he respects and has offered oil to, and been refused,
and does not understand why. Garrow (`charter_garrow`) carts his stone; he
has not asked where from.

**Threads.** T45 (the fourth duration; his stakes are the Charter's side of
it). T50 (the forty stones are in his dike).

**Speaks.**
> "Water goes where it is let. I am not letting it go across the road any
> more. Where it goes instead is a fair question and it has a fair answer,
> which is: not across the road."
> "The Company broke the sluice. A wet road is a paying road and a dry one
> is not. You do not need a level to read that."
> "The crofts pay because the ford voted that the crofts pay. I wrote twice.
> I build what is voted. Ask the moot who should have paid and they will
> tell you the crofts, because they voted it."

### Brede — toll-warden of the fen waist, the bar
**Actor:** the `first_road_toll` bandit_camp's toll-warden, crowned via
`names[]` as **Brede**; talk through a `company_tollwarden` actor def if the
third offer in `q_the_old_gate` needs a tree, else barks plus the offer node
(Band 7 decides; the bible does not) · **Trees:** `brede_bar` (if built),
the "carry it for a cut" node

**Life.** A Company man from the Low Hall, paid on the day, sent up to
hold the bar where the First Road has water on one side and him on the
other because he can count and does not raise his voice. He has held it two
years. He keeps a war table as a counter, a board of receipts, a cage with a
Charter drover in it who could not pay, a line of red-rag stakes down to
the crofts, and a mark-post with six water lines scratched on it a finger
apart, one for every time the water came up over the winter. He waves at
the shoal upstream. They wave back. He has never asked them for anything
and they have never asked him.

**Wants.** The road slow enough to be worth a toll and the towns rich
enough to be worth it. He would say it as: "A road pays or a road is
walked. This one pays. Walk on or pay on."

**Carries.** A croft that would not pay burned in the spring and his crew
says nothing about it, and neither does he, and the not-saying is a stone
in his boot. He keeps the nursery rule to the letter (no Dawnmead door,
nobody under a first sword tolled) and he keeps it the way a man keeps a

## The Standing Course — the Dolmen and the Charter's carter

**The card is §11.5 of the plan and it is law inside every Dolmen line.**

DOES NOT HOLD is the whole judgement vocabulary (true, safe, honest and a
