# VOICE — THE CONTESTED LANDS (cards to merge into VOICE.md §4)

Format as VOICE.md §4: **want / wound / quirk / cadence**, the cadence line
binding. Wit is granted to exactly two throats here (Ammat once, ever;
nobody else) and the Company's Aske inherits the blades' card, which grants
none. Every throat obeys the 2026-08 amendments: whole sentences, hubs of
two or three sentences, answers under about sixty words, no dashes in
anything a player reads, one spice per throat and most throats none. The
full material for every card is in `docs/dialogue-bible/contested-lands.md`.

**The standing boundary holds on every id, name, plaque, bark and comment.**
The palette is fire, stone, ash, bone, the cold, the deep, gloom, grudges,
and things that were here first.

### The green (the Contested Lands cast at Dawnmead)

- **Margit** (Charter tally-clerk, the tally stall): wants the Charter's
  number posted where anyone can read it. Wound: her tally has found that
  the Copse cannot heat Dawnmead and the Third Stone both, and it is not
  her column. Quirk: says "posted" the way other people say "true". Cadence:
  a clerk's, counts first and names after, whole level sentences, never
  dry. Wit: NO.
  > "Carts turned at the fen waist this week: four. Lamps out past the
  > gate: two. That is the Charter's count and it is posted so you can
  > argue with it."
- **Hilde** (Returner widow, the bell bench at noon): wants the Old Road
  lit to the delf by anybody's oil. Wound: Audun died of exactly the thing
  the order says a Returner lamp is; the dark lamp at her door. Quirk:
  reads the oil slate aloud at noon so no name is whispered. Cadence:
  steady, spoken as if to a green full of people, one sentence of feeling
  and then a number. Wit: NO.
  > "I read the names at noon so nobody has to say them quietly. If your
  > name is on it, it is on it out loud, and that is the whole of the
  > shame."
- **The Fenside crofters** (pooled, three bodies, no names): want the mere
  let down by the old gate, the crossing free, their corn carried. Wound:
  under sacking with sheep on a common nobody gave them. Quirk: say "the
  wall folk" openly, the only throats who do. Cadence: three voices in one
  pool (the woman who cooks: weather first, flat; the young man at the
  pen: numbers and animals; the man at the gate: short, tired, will not be
  lectured about grass). Barks only. Wit: NO.
  > "The water came up in the night. We did not hear it. You do not hear
  > water, that is the trouble with it."
- **Leif** (Hale's lamp-boy, the green at midday): wants to walk the lamps
  himself when he is a whole hand. Wound: the barrow in the ditch was his
  family's. Quirk: counts a barrow as a cart because it had a wheel; says
  "the lamp stays lit" like a lock clicking. Cadence: a busy boy's, short
  and exact, present tense, a count in every sentence. Wit: NO.
  > "Three carts and a barrow. The barrow counts. It had a wheel and it
  > had corn in it and it went in the ditch, so it counts."
- **Steinar** (Charter survey lead, billeted at the Five Stones): wants the
  seam chained and the cart road cut by the cold. Wound: his chain crossed
  a kerb and he has decided paper wins. Quirk: everything is a lot number.
  Cadence: ruled lines, bearings and lots, declaratives that do not bend,
  a pause before the word "goblins". Wit: NO.
  > "A kerb is stones. A concession is paper with a seal on it. I have
  > never seen stones win that argument, and I have surveyed for thirty
  > years."

### The First Road east

- **Ingram** (dike-master, the causeway head): wants a dry road to the ford
  by the cold, paid for by the people it keeps dry. Wound: his dike drowns
  the people it bills and he wrote twice to say so. Quirk: reads water out
  loud like a level. Cadence: a builder's, in loads and levels and where
  water goes, blunt and fair and tired of being fair. Wit: NO.
  > "Water goes where it is let. I am not letting it go across the road
  > any more."
- **Brede** (Company toll-warden, the bar at the fen waist; crowned): wants
  the road slow enough to be worth it. Wound: a croft that would not pay
  burned and nobody says so. Quirk: six water lines a finger apart on his
  post, and he is wrong about what they mean. Cadence: Ferrick's courtesy
  copied by a man who is not Ferrick, arithmetic diction, bookkeeping as
  feeling; the closest anyone comes to the spine and it is a bill. Wit: NO
  (his one near-line, "a bill nobody sent", is bookkeeping and is spent).
  > "Six lines on that post, a finger apart. That is the winter. The water
  > came up like a bill nobody sent, and I am the one standing here when
  > it arrives."
- **Halvor** (Fenside headman, the drowned crofts): wants the old gate open
  and the crossing free. Wound: he has seen the kelp-string and knows the
  shoal paid, which makes his son's axe the first wrong; he says nothing.
  Quirk: will not pay the levy and will not stop asking for the dike, and
  does not hear it. Cadence: a headman's, loud about the shoal, short about
  everything else, stops dead near the boy. Wit: NO.
  > "I will not pay for a dike that drowns me. I want the gate open. I
  > know what those two things sound like together. Say them to the water."

### The trail north

- **Sergeant Torsten** (Waykeeper, the fork waystation): wants the First
  Road lamped, the husk burnt, the Old Road left dark. Wound: right about a
  specific death (Roald's crew, past his lamp at dusk) and it has not
  helped. Quirk: a slate with "ours three" and a line through the three.
  Cadence: the Waykeeper card at its hardest, report-speak with the
  understatement taken out, the password said like a lock turning; quotes
  the doctrine by its line and by the envoy who wrote it. Wit: NO.
  > "I told them the count. Roald said the Company was paid for the road.
  > I said the road did not know that. He walked. I have nothing else
  > about it."
- **The Even Court sentinels** (pooled, two, the waystone glade; unnamed):
  want the dying stand left standing until it has finished dying. Wound:
  they let a feller walk into the pack with the words in the wrong tongue.
  Quirk: the wolves walk their thread and nothing else does. Cadence: the
  Evenfall card's four prohibitions plus the plan's fifth: ONE line of the
  common tongue per body per visit, a count or a direction or a refusal or
  a fact about wood, never an aphorism; the rest is the old tongue,
  untranslated, never new. Wit: NO.
  > "The thread is cut in four places. The fourth is new."
- **Bodil** (Charter feller-boss, the licensed cut): wants the lot cut and
  her crew home with the same number of hands. Wound: a feller thirty feet
  in with two sentinels in earshot. Quirk: a tidy thief leaves knucklebones
  on her rope. Cadence: a sawyer's daughter's, cordwood first, fair to the
  wood and unfair to the old folk in one sentence each. Wit: NO.
  > "Up to the thread is the licence. Past the thread is dying anyway."

### The Old Road south

- **Eskil** (keeper of the Third Stone): wants the road lit to the delf and
  the holes filled with whatever is in them. Wound: the lamps do not hold
  and the subscription is the poorest's. Quirk: the slate in chalk because
  the number changes down; says "the diggers", never the other word.
  Cadence: a walker's, distances in stones, counts that go down, one hard
  sentence about the holes and no softer one. Wit: NO.
  > "Kingsdelf, forty-one stones, nine lit. The slate is chalk because the
  > number changes. It changes down more than up. I keep writing it."
- **Aske** (Company escort-man, the toll stone): wants to be paid to keep
  the dark road. Wound: Roald, one of four mounds with a red rag. Quirk:
  "paid for the road, not for under it"; will pay for eleven gnolls and
  not learn their names. Cadence: the Company blades' card, house-rule
  brevity inside whole sentences, warmth granted as brazier space. Wit: NO.
  > "We are paid for the road. Not for under it. The hole is the road's
  > problem and the road can take it up with the diggers."

### The wold west

- **Rurik** (Crown chainman, the stakes across the wold): wants the west
  way in the Crown's book. Wound: entered a people that was farming as a
  hostile encampment because that is the word the book has. Quirk: reads
  his own entries back as answers. Cadence: Hedda's chainman, distances
  first, "forty-one paces", no adjectives, a column for everything and a
  compliment for nothing. Wit: NO.
  > "I have entered you as a witness. It is not a compliment. It is a
  > column."
- **Grubb Turnsoil** (Doorless chief; crowned; barks only): wants the ash
  and the wold's edge to farm in, fire for the beds. Wound: came up through
  a grave. Quirk: a knucklebone on the rail for everything taken. Cadence:
  goblin, present tense, every sentence a thing you can hold, "paid" for a
  bone, never why they came up, never another people's name. Wit: NO.
  > "One sheep. One bone on the rail. Paid."
- **Harguk Fiveblows** (Legion drillmaster; crowned; barks only): wants
  every goblin on the wold under the crimson square by the next moon.
  Wound: none he would call one. Quirk: the banner does not vary. Cadence:
  parade-ground declaratives, ranks and paces, never a boast that is not
  also a fact, never a threat twice. Wit: NO.
  > "Ranks of eight. Deserters to the rear. The rear is where deserters go."

### The Standing Course

**THE DOLMEN CARD (plan §11.5, verbatim; law inside every Dolmen line).**
The first non-human people on a non-human rig who talk. Common tongue
learned over eleven summers from Fenside drovers, so their words are
crofter words. THE SET SENTENCE: short level declaratives laid one on the
last. HOLDS / DOES NOT HOLD is their entire judgement vocabulary (true,
safe, honest and a good person all "bear"; a debt is "weight"). COUNT
BEFORE OPINION and they NEVER ASK A QUESTION. No names for topsiders
(the stake man, the chain man, the sheep man, the lamp boys, the red-rag
men). THE WET CAME UP is rain, flood and the water-table in one phrase.
"We set here." is the whole answer to why they came up. Spice: Ammat only,
once; the spine never gestured at (she may say a stone moved; never that
something is moving).

*The retired-word note.* The people were "the Tholl" in the plan's first
draft and were renamed the Dolmen on 2026-09-04 because Tholl is a homophone
of the bar's word in an epic about bars. That word never stands inside a
Dolmen line: the stile is a stile, the stone is a stone, and Brede is the
one who names their thing with his own word ("a stile-price") and laughs.
The strata's first names were retired the same day (Hesket for Northguard's
Hesk, the Whin for the live actor Wyn, Kesk one letter from Hesk, Oskel
beside Eskil); the names now are Ammat, Drusa, Durrow, Sarsen, Vorl
Fullweight, and the carter is Garrow.

- **Ammat** (coursemother of the Marl, the north lip): wants the Course
  finished to the bend and the forty back or forty like them. Wound: her
  count of stones moved (nine, fourteen, thirty-three) is the truest
  measurement in the valley and she has filed it under bad setting. Quirk:
  a count for the laugh of anyone who laughs. Cadence: the set sentence at
  its most patient; the one Dolmen throat that will sit with you. Wit:
  YES, once, ever ("it does not mind weight, it minds wrong weight"), spent
  in the first tree.
  > "The stake man took forty stones from the north end and carried them
  > to his wet road. We counted them going. We will have forty back, or
  > forty like them. That is not a threat. It is a count."
- **Sarsen** (the Marl's setter at the dry end): wants the cairn set right
  in the morning and the sheep counted on and off. Wound: he says the
  disowning of Vorl's stile to the people who just paid it. Quirk: has
  learned that topsiders sit down when they are tired. Cadence: the set
  sentence with the most crofter in it; the first Dolmen a player meets.
  Wit: NO.
  > "The small diggers stack this every night with one stone wrong. I set
  > it right every morning. Neither of us has said anything about it. It
  > is a good arrangement."
- **Drusa** (of the Sinter, the wet floor): wants the wet floor left wet.
  Wound: moved for water that came up under them, the same as the shoal,
  and neither knows. Quirk: speaks only as "we"; grinds stone on stone in
  the idle. Cadence: a few sentences each, the water refusal as a count,
  the slowest throat in the game. Wit: NO.
  > "The last dry course is the ninth. We stop at the ninth."
- **Durrow** (of the Culm, the hearth-cells): wants stone that burns kept
  burning and a buyer who asks in words. Wound: hot and quick in a level
  people. Quirk: the only oath any Dolmen has, "cold on you". Cadence: the
  set sentence at speed, the one Dolmen voice with heat in it, the oath as
  a goodbye. Wit: NO.
  > "Cold on you, then. Go and be cold."
- **Vorl Fullweight** (champion of the Gossan, the weight-yard; crowned;
  barks only): wants the weight kept and a wall set for the stake man in
  exchange for forty. Wound: none; the weight is kept without wanting.
  Quirk: unpicks every stake but the chain man's. Cadence: the weight
  recited as inventory, the stile law in six words. Wit: NO.
  > "One cart. Twelve wheels of stone. A rope. A row of sticks with glass
  > on them. That is the weight and I keep it."
- **Garrow** (Charter carter-boss, the stone-yard; human): wants six wains
  moving and the dike fed by the cold. Wound: he knows the bank was a
  course and has decided it is not his column. Quirk: "I have a schedule"
  as an answer to everything. Cadence: a carter's, loads and schedules,
  the same three words to end every hard sentence. Wit: NO.
  > "They do not come to the yard. They do not ask. I have a schedule.
  > Those three things together are what a carter calls a field."

## Review checklist additions for this cast

1. Any Dolmen line with a question mark, a topsider's name, or the bar's
   word in it: rewrite.
2. Any Dolmen line where a count comes after an opinion: swap them.
3. Any throat saying why the water came up, why the wolves came down, why
   the stones moved, or why the Doorless came up, as a cause and not a
   local reading: cut. Brede's bill is the ceiling.
4. Any of the thirteen contradictions in `contested-lands.md` reconciled
   by a line, a sign, a journal entry or a flag: revert.
5. Any sentinel with two common-tongue lines in one visit, or one that
   would be true in a fortune cookie: cut to the count.
6. grep the diff for the boundary words and for dashes in player-facing
   text, as VOICE.md §6 says.
