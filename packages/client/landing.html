The Long Dark

The key is the dungeon

Dungeons in Arx are cut by their keys. A rift-cut key carries a place inside it: the same key always opens the same halls, and its ward holds three turns before it wears through. Six kinds of dark to cut into — cavern, mine, stronghold, crypt, warren, heartwood — with a champion standing between you and the chest.

Nothing respawns behind you. Clear a hall and it stays cleared until the key wears out.

Step into a friend’s run mid-fight and the door stays open to the party.

The Wild at Heel

Sixteen species will walk beside you

Wolf, bear, lynx, great owl, worg among them. A companion fights at your side, levels through its own deeds, and falls without ever being lost. Gentling one is its own craft — beastcraft climbs like any other skill, by doing it.

And when the road is long: the Dawnlands courser, the shaggy Hoargate garron, the night sabercat. You ride to cross the world; the moment you fight, your boots are back on the ground.

Drawn, not drawn on. That key is not an image file — it is the game’s own painter, running in this page, in your browser, right now. So is the meadow further down. Nothing on this page is concept art.

The Spoken Land

Eight towns that keep their own day

A hundred and eighty-eight characters are cast and voiced, and every one of them has somewhere to be. The smith is at the anvil in the morning and in the inn at night; the watch changes on its own hours; the market is open when the market is open. Two of them may tell you different truths about the same thing. Leave with both.

2,293 lines of dialogue. Six factions keeping books on you, from hunted to champion — prices, fines, work and doors all follow the name you carry.

The Tended Earth

Or never draw a sword at all

Twenty-three crops, livestock that learn to trust your hands, eighty buildable pieces that persist in the shared world, and seven named trades that each go all the way to mastery. What you raise stays raised — and only you can pull your own walls down.

Ore veins deplete and come back for everyone. A house someone raised last winter is still standing when you walk past it tomorrow.
